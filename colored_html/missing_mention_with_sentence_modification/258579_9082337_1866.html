Helen Beatrix Potter (28 July 1866 – 22 December 1943) was an English writer, illustrator, mycologist and conservationist.[Helen Beatrix Potter (July 28, 1866 – December 22, 1943) was an English writer, illustrator, mycologist and conservationist.] She wrote several classic children's storybooks including The Tale of Peter Rabbit.

Life

Potter was born on 28 July 1866 in South Kensington, England.[1] The family was wealthy. She spent most of her holidays in Scotland and the Lake District. There, she learned to love nature, plants and animals. She also sketched and painted.[2]

Potter started her career making drawings for greeting cards. In 1900 she wrote and drew the pictures for the children's storybook The Tale of Peter Rabbit. It was very successful. Potter wrote more than 20 children's books during her life.[3] She also loved painting plants and animals. She was particularly good at making scientific illustrations of fungi (mushrooms).[4]

Because children liked her stories, Potter sold a doll in the shape of Peter Rabbit. She also used her illustrations for children's dishes and clothing. These items were also very successful and Potter earned a lot of money from them.[4]

Potter married William Heelis in 1913.[3] They were married until Potter died in 1943. The couple did not have any children. In 1923 Potter bought a sheep farm in the Lake District of England. There she raised Herdwick sheep.[5] She left most of her money to the National Trust.

Potter died on 22 December 1943 in the village of Near Sawrey in England.[1]
