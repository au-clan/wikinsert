June Rosalind Brocksom CBE (née Spencer; born 14 June 1919)[1][2] is an English retired actress. She is best known as the voice of Peggy Woolley on the BBC Radio 4 radio soap opera The Archers.[3]

Spencer celebrated her 100th birthday in 2019.[4] She announced her retirement on 8 August 2022, at the age of 103, after 79 years in the industry.[5]

Early life

June Rosalind Spencer was born at her parents home on Bingham Road in Sherwood, Nottingham, England on 14 June 1919.[1][6] She is the only child[7] of Rosalind Mary[8] (née Thorne),[9] a housewife, and[10] William Spencer,[11][12] a salesman for Crawford's.[13] She was baptised on 3 August 1919 at St John's the Evangelist in nearby Carrington, Nottingham.[14] Spencer lived on Bingham Road until the age of seven.[15]

Spencer attended Mountford House Preparatory School from 1924 to 1929. She graduated from Nottingham High School for Girls in 1934, at the age of 15, after being told "you'll never make anything of yourself without your certificate" by Miss Phillips, her headmistress.[16] She studied part-time at the Stockwin Music College from the age of 12.[17] Spencer was a member of the Brownies.[18] She joined the St Mary's Amateur Dramatic Society in 1942, where she gained her London Guildhall School of Music and Drama certificate.[19][20]

Spencer made her stage debut at the age of three, during a performance of the King of the Land of Nod.[21][22] She took piano lessons for 11 years, starting at the age of five. She later passed the Associated Board's Advanced Level.[23][19] Spencer also wrote and produced plays in her back garden to raise money for charity from the age of 12.[22] Her charity of choice was always Dr Barnardo's.[24]

Spencer was taught how to drive by her father at the age of 12, while she was underage and uninsured. He also taught her how to drive through water. Her lessons came to an abrupt stop when her father discovered that one of his friends had been reprimanded for teaching his schoolboy son. She passed her official driving test the second time.[25]

Spencer paid £110 for her first car, a 1934 Standard 12, which she named 'Aggie'. Her car had no heating so she would drive with a hot-water bottle on her knees and a blanket over the top. She sold Aggie for £120 two years later.[25]

Spencer worked as a model, modelling teenage clothes in fashion shows for two local stores, earning "a few guineas". She also worked as a junior governess at a small private school.[26] Spencer and her father lectured at the Mansfield Technical College. She was engaged for two terms.[27][28]

Career

Spencer applied for auditions at the BBC's Midland region during the late summer of 1939, passing both auditions for drama and review.[29] Her first paid work was performing monologues at Freemasons' dinners in 1942.[30] She reapplied four years later, passing both auditions again.[29]

Spencer made her broadcasting debut in November 1943, when she was cast as a 12-year-old child in a 15-minute programme, Railways in Wartime.[7]

Spencer was contracted to complete a season in weekly rep theatre, with two shows a day for three guineas a week. The arrangement broke down when she demanded a pay rise for taking the title role in a Christmas production of Alice in Wonderland, with her boss suggesting that she should be grateful to have war work. She immediately quit, telling her boss: "I'd rather fill shells than work for you".[4] Her first line on the professional stage was "What's your Will?" from A Midsummer Night's Dream.[31]

Spencer worked the telephone switchboards as a "hello girl" while volunteering with the Voluntary Entertainment company performing in morale-raising shows for the forces in her spare time.[32]

Spencer joined the cast of the BBC Radio 4 radio soap opera The Archers on 29 May 1950. She was the long-standing voice of Margaret "Peggy" Woolley (née Perkins, formally Archer) for 63 years, from the pilot episode until 1953, and then again from 1962 until her departure on 31 July 2022.[3][33][34] She was the sole survivor from the original cast.[22] Spencer did not audition for the role as she was already contracted to the BBC to star in a string of other radio plays.[35] She was originally paid £12 for five episodes - as an experienced actress - while others were paid between £8 and £10.[36] She publicly criticised the creator, Godfrey Baseley,[37] who had once threatened to sack her after she decided to not attend his party.[38] Spencer left the serial in 1953 to raise her two children and the role of Peggy was recast to Thelma Rogers.[30] She also voiced other characters including a Scottish maid and an Irish baker's assistant called Rita Flynn. She once played two of her characters in a single scene.[39] Spencer was credited under her married name of June Brocksom on nine episodes between 28 November 1969 and 9 August 1973, voicing Mrs Spenlowe.[40] She had gained widespread acclaim in the 2000s for her part in a storyline that portrayed the slow submission of Peggy's husband, Jack Woolley, to Alzheimer's disease.[41][42][43] She had criticised the shows pay in 2011.[44] Spencer had recorded episodes at the Chelsea Flower Show.[3] She was driven to and from her home on each recording day in her later years.[31] Spencer and Camilla, Duchess of Cornwall cut an Archers-themed cake during a reception at Clarence House to celebrate the show's 70th anniversary on 7 December 2021.[45]

Spencer was approached to join the BBC's Repertory Company.[46][47]

Spencer wrote a number of the "Odd Odes" performed by Cyril Fletcher on That's Life!.[27]

Spencer portrayed Marian Hennessy in the 2 November 2000 episode of the BBC One medical soap opera Doctors.[48]

Spencer was the guest on the 28 February 2010 episode of the BBC Radio 4 programme Desert Island Discs,[36] and the subject on the 15 May 2011 episode of the BBC Radio 4 Extra programme Desert Island Discs Revisited.[49]

Spencer announced her retirement on 8 August 2022, at the age of 103, after 79 years in the industry. She said: "In 1950, I helped to plant an acorn. It took root and in January 1951 it was planted out and called The Archers. Over the years it has thrived and become a splendid great tree with many branches. But now this old branch, known as Peggy, has become weak and unsafe so I decided it was high time she 'boughed' out, so I have duly lopped her."[5][50] She was the longest serving radio actress in the United Kingdom.[51] Spencer had made previous attempts to retire. She said: "I've been trying to retire for at least a year. They didn't want to lose her character. Every time I tried to stop, they gave me more episodes."[52][53]

Personal life

Spencer first met Roger Sidney Brocksom while on holiday in Chapel-St-Leonards, Lincolnshire at the age of 17.[54] The couple were married on 27 May 1942.[55] The couple adopted two children, a son and a daughter.[56] Her husband died from a stroke in 26 May 2001.[57] Her son, David, died in Nuremberg, Germany in 2006.[58]

Spencer currently resides in a Care UK care home.[59] Shepreviously resided in Leatherhead, Surrey.[Spencer currently resides in a Care UK care home.[51] She previously resided in a bungalow in Leatherhead, Surrey.]

Spencer has been a patron of Alzheimer's Research UK since 2005.[60][61] She has volunteered for clinical trials in tests for Alzheimer's disease.[62] Spencer is a Christian.[23] She said: "I'm not, sort of, deeply into religion, but I do have faith."[63]

Spencer celebrated her 100th birthday in 2019.[4]

Honours

Spencer was appointed Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in the 1991 Birthday Honours[64] and Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in the 2017 Birthday Honours by Queen Elizabeth II[65] for her services to drama and charity.[66]

Spencer, along with Arnold Peters and The Archers, won a Mental Health BAFTA for Jack and Peggy's Alzheimer's story in 2007. Spencer and Peters collected the award.[62]

Spencer received the Freedom of the City of London in June 2010.[67][68]

Spencer recieved an honorary degree from the University of Nottingham as a Doctor of Letters for her services to broadcasting in July 2012.[69][70]

Spencer received the lifetime achievement award for 63 years of service at the BBC Audio Drama Awards in January 2014.[22][71][72]

Spencer received the GDST Exceptional Contribution award from Nottingham Girls' High School in July 2022.[16][73][74]
