Patricia Honor Richardson MBE (née Greene; born January 1931)[1] is an English actress. She is best known as the voice of Jill Archer on the BBC Radio 4 radio soap opera The Archers.[2]

Greene is one of the world's longest-serving actors in a soap opera, in any medium.[3]

Early life

Patricia Honor Greene was born in Allenton, Derby, England in January 1932.[1][4] She is the youngest of two children of Josie Greene (née Johnson), a housewife, and Edward Greene, an engineer and former piano salesman.[5] She has an older sister.[6] Greene and her family moved to Champion Street in the New Zealand area, and then later moved to Kirk Street in Chester Green.[7]

Greene attended Ashgate Infants School on Ashbourne Road and St Paul's Junior School. She was also educated at Parkfields Cedars Grammar School.[7] She joined an amateur dramatic society. Greene trained at the Royal Central School of Speech and Drama in London from 1951 to 1954.[4]

Greene worked as a ward orderly at the Derbyshire Children's Hospital. She worked as a secretary in the sheet metal factory of Hawk Industries. She also worked as a bus conductress on the trolley bus in Derby, and as a waitress during the holidays.[4]

Greene once noticed her father crying at one of her theatre performances.--{performances} She credited that moment with her wanting to become an actress as she wanted to make people express emotions.[8]

Greene has recalled experiences of her mother having an extramarital affair, in which Josie would bring the man to their family home and order Patricia not to tell her father. She said that it was not the best way to bring up a child.[5]

Career

Greene made her broadcasting debut in 1957, after being told "you'll never broadcast" by a tutor.[4]

Greene joined the cast of the BBC Radio 4 radio soap opera The Archers on 25 July 1957.[9] She is the long-standing voice of Jill Archer (née Patterson).[2] She was the sixth, and last, actress to audition and thought she would not get the role. Greene found the first six weeks tough due to her inexperience. She recalled that on her second day, she took stage directions too far and threw a glass of water over Norman Painting, who portrayed Phil Archer, nearly and electrocuted him. This impressed the creator Godfrey Baseley and he kept her in the serial.[8] She was paid seven guineas a week.[4] Greene was tempted to leave the series when her son was a toddler but felt a loyalty to the programme and stayed.[8] She was paid £16,000 for her work on the series in 2017.[10]

Greene portrayed Margaret Richmond in the first episode of the BBC medical soap opera Doctors on 26 March 2000. She reprised the role for a second time in the 20 April 2000 episode.[11]

Greene portrayed Ivy Watson in the 22 October 2000 episode of the BBC One medical drama Casualty.[11]

Greene was the guest on the 27 December 2015 episode of the BBC Radio 4 programme Desert Island Discs.[5][12]

Charity work

Greene is a patron of the Macular Society.[13]

Personal life

Greene married actor Henry George Selway in October 1959 in Maidenhead, Berkshire. The couple divorced a few years later.[14][5]

Greene married Cyril Austen Richardson in April 1972 in Windsor, Berkshire.[15] The couple have one son.[16] She was widowed in November 1986.[17]

Honours

Greene was appointed Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE) in 1997 Birthday Honours by Queen Elizabeth II for services to radio drama.[18]

Greene recieved an honorary degree from the University of Derby as a Master of Arts in 2017.[4]
