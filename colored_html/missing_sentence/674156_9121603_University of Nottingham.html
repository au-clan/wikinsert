June Rosalind Spencer CBE (born 14 June 1919)[1] is an English retired actress. She is best known for playing Peggy Woolley in the BBC Radio 4 soap opera The Archers.

Spencer became a centenarian on 14 June 2019.[2] She announced her retirement on 8 August 2022, at the age of 103.[3]

Early life

June Rosalind Spencer was born on 14 June 1919, in Nottingham, Nottinghamshire, England[1] as the only child to Rosalind Mary Spencer (née Thorne) and William Spencer.[4] She was baptised on 3 August 1919 at St John's Church in Carrington, Nottingham.[5]

Career

Spencer made her broadcasting debut in 1943.[6]

Spencer joined the BBC Radio 4 soap opera The Archer in May 1950. She portrayed the role of Peggy Woolley for 72 years, from the pilot episode until her departure from the series on 31 July 2022. She was the sole survivor from the original cast.[6] Spencer has also portrayed other characters including a Scottish maid and an Irish baker's assistant called Rita Flynn. She once portrayed two of her characters in a single scene.[7]

Spencer portrayed Marian Hennessy in an episode of the BBC One soap opera Doctors in 2000.[8]

Spencer was the guest on the 28 February 2010 episode of the BBC Radio 4 programme Desert Island Discs.[9]

Personal life

Spencer met Roger Sidney Brocksom in 1936, at the age of 17. The couple were married in 1942 in Nottingham, Nottinghamshire.[10] They adopted two children, a son and a daughter.[11] Brocksom died from a stroke in 2001. He was 81.[12] Brocksom had suffered with Alzheimer's disease for 10 years prior to his death and Spencer had served as his carer.[13][11] Their son David died from alcoholism in 2005. He was 55.[14]

Spencer currently resides in a bungalow in Leatherhead, Surrey.[15]

Spencer sponsors a child in Thailand who calls her 'Granny June'.[11]

Honours

Spencer was appointed Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in 1991 and Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in the 2017 Birthday Honours for services to drama and charity.[16]

Spencer received the Freedom of the City of London in June 2010.[17][[Spencer was awarded an honorary degree from the University of Nottingham as a Doctor of Letters for her services to broadcasting in July 2012.[20]]]

Spencer received the lifetime achievement award at the BBC Audio Drama Awards in January 2014.[18]

Spencer received the GDST Exceptional Contribution award from Nottingham Girls' High School in July 2022.[19]
